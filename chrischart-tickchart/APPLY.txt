TICK CHART — what to copy and what to run
=========================================

Copy these over ~/Downloads/chrischart, keeping the paths:

    liqmap/tickbar.py       NEW   the N-print bar builder
    liqmap/cme_tape.py            feeds the builders from the raw tape
    liqmap/web.py                 the route, the selector, the x-axis maths
    tests/test_tickbar.py   NEW   84 tests
    tests/test_clockwin.py        one test's source anchor, see below

Then:

    cd ~/Downloads/chrischart
    source .venv/bin/activate
    python -m pytest tests/ -q          # 5,377 passed here
    git add -A
    git commit -m "tick bars: N-print candles on the chart"
    git push origin main

WHY test_clockwin.py IS IN HERE
-------------------------------
One test located a block in cme_tape.py by the exact text of its `if`
line. The tick bars share that block's decode of the raw record, so the
condition gained a term and the anchor stopped matching -- with the
test's actual claim still true. The fix is to the anchor only; nothing
that test asserts has changed.


HOW TO USE IT
-------------
The chart's candle selector now has a second group: "by prints (ES tape
only)". Pick a size and the same chart draws N-print bars instead of
time bars. 233 / 500 / 1,000 / 1,597 / 2,000 / 4,000 are all counting
from the moment the engine starts, so switching between them is instant
rather than starting an empty chart.

Under the chart you now get the pace instead of the trade count:

    1000 prints a bar · on pace for 41s against a 96s median --
    the tape is roughly twice as busy

That line is the thing a clock chart cannot show you. It compares the
bar being built -- PROJECTED to a full bar, not its raw elapsed time --
against the median of the last 30 closed bars.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO, SAID PLAINLY
-----------------------------------
IT DOES NOT CHANGE WHEN THE RULE FIRES.

All three strategies still read the 15-minute (or 5-minute) window.
Swapping the chart underneath them changes what you are looking at and
nothing about what the auto trader is doing. If the entries were late
yesterday they will be late today.

The reason to put the tick series in first is that it is the thing a
faster rule would have to read, and it has to exist and be counting
before anything can be measured on it. Wiring it into the entry is a
separate change and a deliberate one -- say the word and it becomes a
fourth strategy.

Two honest notes on the "tick charts lead" idea:

  * A tick bar holds no information a time bar lacks. It is the same
    prints, cut differently. What it changes is the CLOSING SCHEDULE --
    a rule reading tick bars can act at the Nth print, while the same
    rule on a 15m candle cannot act until :15. That is where any lead
    comes from, and it is real, but it is not magic.

  * Faster bars means more bars means more entries means more round
    trips. ES costs about a tick a round trip. A rule that fires six
    times as often needs to be more than six times as right to come
    out ahead, and nothing has measured that yet.


WHAT IS DELIBERATELY BLANK
--------------------------
  * No trade markers on a tick chart. Your trades are recorded against
    a TIME bar's timestamp, so drawing them here would put each one on
    whichever tick bar happened to span that second -- a marker that
    looks placed and is not.
  * No volume profile and no projection on a tick series. The
    projection is a distance over a remaining duration, and a tick bar
    has no scheduled end to have a remainder of.
  * No countdown on the forming bar, for the same reason.
  * The read/stack selector will NOT follow the chart onto a tick size,
    even with "follow" ticked. The stack needs ten closed bars of a
    clock interval; handing it "1000t" would not switch it to tick
    bars, it would make every row unreadable.
